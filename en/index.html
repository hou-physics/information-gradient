Part III Trunk Two

Chapters 13–15, trunk two, ask how people read and use information, from disagreement and copying others (Chapters 13 and 14) to what holders of information choose to tell (Chapter 15).

  1. 13Disagreement, Persuasion and Biased Beliefs
  2. 14Information Cascades and Herding
  3. 15The Sender’s Choices, Sharing and Disclosure

Part IV The Canopy

Chapters 16–18, the canopy, ask how information enters prices, from what traders learn from prices (Chapter 16) to what kind of probability a price is (Chapter 18).

  1. 16Learning from Prices
  2. 17Insiders and Market Makers
  3. 18Price-Implied Probabilities and Risk Premium

Part V Bridges

Chapters 19–21, the bridges, hold ready-made models that join the two trunks, such as gradual information diffusion together with momentum, the tendency of past winners to keep rising and past losers to keep falling (Chapter 20); they come after the canopy because they use its tools. They also cover getting information to where the capital is (Chapter 19) and beliefs about what others believe (Chapter 21).

  1. 19Word of Mouth and Rational Expectations
  2. 20Bad News, Short-Sale Constraints and Momentum
  3. 21Higher-Order Beliefs, Bubbles and Coordination

Part VI The Fruit

Chapters 22–24, the fruit, ask which gradients can be traded: crypto assets such as Bitcoin (Chapter 22), the decay of published anomalies (Chapter 23) and a map of the evidence with a checkup for trading edges (Chapter 24).

  1. 22Crypto Assets
  2. 23Anomaly Decay and Limits to Arbitrage
